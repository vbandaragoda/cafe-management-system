# Marketing Plan — Caffora

This is a coursework marketing plan for a real, working product prototype.
Numbers below are framed as **goals/targets**, not measured outcomes —
Caffora has not launched to real customers, so no real usage data exists
yet.

## Target users

1. **Students and young professionals near campus/office cafes** who want
   to order ahead or skip a counter queue between classes/meetings — the
   core dine-in and pickup customer, served primarily through the mobile
   app's table-QR-scan flow.
2. **Cafe owners/managers** running a single independent cafe or small
   chain, who want less counter congestion, a digital order queue instead
   of shouted tickets, and basic sales visibility — served through the
   admin console (available on both web and mobile).
3. **Occasional/guest customers** who don't want to create an account just
   to look at a menu — served by GUEST-level public browsing before any
   registration is required.

## Problem and value proposition

Small independent cafes typically run on a single till and a paper/verbal
order queue. At peak times this creates a visible line, mis-heard orders,
and no easy way for the owner to see what's selling. Caffora's value
proposition:

- **For customers:** scan the table, browse a real menu with live
  availability, order and pay without flagging down staff, and track order
  status on your own phone instead of waiting near the counter.
- **For cafe staff/owners:** one shared order queue (web or mobile) that
  updates the moment a customer orders, structured order data instead of
  handwritten tickets, and real sales/top-product reports instead of
  guesswork — computed directly from the same order data (see
  `docs/database/erd.md` and the reporting endpoints), not a separate
  spreadsheet kept in sync by hand.

## Channels

- **In-cafe table-tent QR codes.** This is Caffora's most distinctive
  channel because it is **both** the ordering mechanism **and** a
  marketing touchpoint: every table becomes a first-touch acquisition
  surface for a customer who has never used the app before, with no ad
  spend required — the QR code is generated by the admin (`qr_flutter`)
  and printed on a table tent.
- **Campus/local social media** (a cafe's existing Instagram/TikTok/campus
  group-chat presence) to announce "order ahead" as a feature, with short
  clips of the QR-scan-to-order flow.
- **Local partnerships** with student unions, coworking spaces, or campus
  event organizers to place Caffora table-tents at pop-up/event coffee
  stands, extending reach beyond the cafe's four walls.
- **Loyalty/referral via "Invite a friend."** The mobile app's explicit
  stored-contacts feature (`flutter_contacts`, `contacts_service.dart`) is
  reused as a low-friction referral channel: a customer already in the app
  can invite a specific contact directly, rather than a generic share link
  that gets lost in a chat thread.

## Launch strategy

1. **Soft launch at a single pilot cafe** with table-tent QR codes on every
   table and staff trained on the admin console — validates the core loop
   (scan → order → kitchen fulfils → customer picks up/gets served) before
   any wider promotion.
2. **In-cafe-only promotion first** (staff mentioning it, table tents) to
   build a base of real orders and catch usability issues while the
   audience is small and forgiving.
3. **Local/social promotion second**, once the ordering flow has been
   exercised by real customers, to avoid a broken first impression at
   scale.

## Retention ideas

- Order history and one-tap "reorder" from `orders_screen.dart`/
  `CartOrdersPage.jsx`, since dine-in/pickup customers are often repeat
  customers with a small set of regular orders.
- Push the referral ("Invite a friend") prompt after a customer's *second*
  completed order, not the first — by then they've had a real positive
  experience to refer, rather than being asked to vouch for something
  untested.
- Light/dark theming and a fast, low-friction QR-first flow are retention
  factors in themselves: the goal is an order taking under a minute from
  scan to confirmation, so returning is low-effort.

## KPIs (goals, not measured results)

| KPI | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Weekly active orderers** | Core adoption signal — are people actually using the app repeatedly, not just once |
| **Repeat-order rate** (customers with ≥2 orders in 30 days) | Measures whether the value proposition holds up after the novelty of scanning a QR code wears off |
| **Average order value** | Indicates whether digital ordering is displacing or growing counter revenue, and whether upsell (e.g. add-ons) is working |
| **QR-scan-to-order conversion rate** | Measures friction in the specific flow this product is built around (diagram (e) in `docs/architecture/sequence-diagrams.md`) — a low conversion rate would point at menu/UX problems, not marketing reach |
| **Referral-driven signups** (via "Invite a friend") | Tests whether the contacts-based referral channel actually produces new users, versus being a feature nobody uses |

No claims are made here about achievable numbers for any of the above —
they are the metrics the team would track post-launch, not projections.
